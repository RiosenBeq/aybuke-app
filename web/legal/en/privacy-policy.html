Privacy at a glance

  • Aybuke is designed with teens in mind, so everyone gets high-privacy defaults: no tracking, no profiling and no public profiles or feeds.
  • Free members see a few ads from Google AdMob: a short ad you can choose to watch for 1 credit when you're out of credits (a few a day), and now and then an ad after you save. Ads are never personalised and never use your photos. Pro has no ads, and there are none under 13.

  • Your library stays on your iPhone. Background removal and manual adjustments never leave your device.
  • Photos and prompts go to the cloud only after you opt in, and only to create the result you asked for. We don't store them, and results are deleted from the cloud within 1 hour.
  • We never use your photos, prompts or results to train AI models, and we never sell them or use them for advertising or marketing.
  • You can switch off cloud AI processing or delete your data and account in the app at any time. If you're under 18, start with section 3.

2. Designed with teens in mind

Aybuke is designed with teen users in mind. That's why we apply high-privacy defaults to everyone who uses it, whatever their age:

  • Few ads, never personalised. Pro members and anyone under 13 see no ads, and neither does the Mac app. Free members see ads from Google AdMob in two places only: a short ad they can choose to watch for 1 credit when they're out of credits (a few a day, as the app shows), and now and then an ad after they save or share a finished creation (never in the first day, at most 3 a day). Ads are never personalised and never use your photos, prompts or results, and teens and anyone whose age we don't know get only ads suitable for a general audience (section 5).
  • No tracking. We don't track you across other companies' apps or websites. The app never asks for permission to track you and doesn't access your device's advertising identifier (IDFA). The only third-party analytics SDK is RevenueCat's subscription analytics, which starts only once you make a purchase, and Google's ad SDK starts only when an ad may be shown to a free member (section 5).
  • No profiling. We don't build profiles about you, and we don't make decisions about you with legal or similarly significant effects based on automated processing.
  • No public profiles or feeds. Nothing about you is public in Aybuke. Your creations are shared only if you choose to share them.
  • No sharing for marketing. We don't sell personal information or share it for personalised (cross-context behavioural) advertising or for marketing, and we don't work with data brokers. We don't send you marketing messages.
  • Your library stays on your device. The creations you keep are stored only on your iPhone.
  • Cloud processing only after you opt in. Nothing leaves your device for one of our cloud AI features until you agree. Apple's Image Playground has its own notice (section 6).
  • No AI training. We never use your photos, prompts or results to train AI models, and we use only AI provider plans whose terms don't allow training on customer content without our permission, which we never give.
  • No face recognition. We don't perform face recognition, create biometric templates or face embeddings, or try to identify the people in your photos.

We follow the standards of the UK Age Appropriate Design Code (the "Children's Code") for all users. Section 18 explains how.

4. Data that stays on your iPhone

Much of Aybuke works without sending anything to us. The following data is stored only on your device, and we can't access it:

  • Your library ("My Works"): the images you keep, with their title, the type of edit, the text prompt you used (for Create from text and Magic Edit), whether you marked them as a favourite, and timestamps. The library is stored in the app's private storage and protected by iOS Data Protection. When you delete a creation, it stays in Recently deleted for 30 days, where you can restore it or delete it for good; after that, it's removed.
  • App settings (stored in iOS UserDefaults): whether you've completed onboarding; the interests you picked (for example "Selfie glow-ups" or "Shots for my shop"), which are used only to order the home screen on your device; your age range (under 13, 13 to 15, 16 to 17, or 18 and over), whether it came from Apple's Declared Age Range or the app's own question, and when it was set (never your birth date); your cloud AI consent choice, when you made it, which version of the consent screen and which processors it named (so we can ask again when they change) and, where asked, your answer to "A parent or guardian said yes"; your appearance preference; your notification choices; the last 10 prompts you made pictures with in Create and the text you're still writing there (you can clear the list in Create); a small count of happy moments (saving, sharing or hearting a creation) and when the app last asked, used only to time Apple's rating prompt; for ads, when you first opened the app, how many creations you've saved, when you last saw an ad and how many you saw today (used only to keep ads rare, section 5) and whether you've asked for an ad on this device; Google's record of your ad consent choices, if you made them (kept on your device by Google's consent tool); and, only in offline or demo mode, a local credit balance.
  • Session tokens for your anonymous account, stored in the iOS Keychain.
  • Reminders: notifications are off until you allow them and switch them on. The app then schedules them locally on your device: a reminder before the cancel-by time of a free trial (at least 26 hours before it ends, earlier if that would fall at night or in school hours); "your edit is ready" when a creation finishes while you're in another app (skipped at night and in school hours; it never quotes your prompt); "your free credits are back" (only after you run out); and, if you choose, a look of the week. Reminders are never sent between 9 pm and 9 am, or during school hours (8 am to 3 pm) on weekdays. No push server or device token is involved.
  • Widgets and Live Activity: to show your credits, your latest creation and its progress on the Home Screen, the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, the app shares a small snapshot (credit balance, the title and a small thumbnail of your latest creation, and how many creations you have) with its widgets through a container on your device. It never leaves your iPhone.

On-device features. Remove background (using Apple's Vision framework, including picking which subject to keep, new backgrounds and stickers), Hide faces, Adjust, Effects (including the Digicam '04 look offered during onboarding), Retro Cam and Photo Booth process your photos entirely on your iPhone.

On-device face checks. These features look at faces, only on your iPhone, with Apple's Vision framework: Best shot ranks the selfies you pick by light, focus and how clearly the face shows; the kindness reminder counts how many people are in a photo; the likeness check compares an AI edit with your original photo (face proportions and skin lightness) so it can offer a free retry if the result drifted; Hide faces finds where faces are so you can blur or cover other people's faces before you share; and the Cover star and Photo strip share designs and Photo Booth prints find where the face is so the crop keeps it in frame. Nothing is stored or sent to us, nobody is identified or recognised, and nothing rates anyone's looks.

On-device writing help. On devices with Apple Intelligence, the idea and prompt suggestions in Create ("Inspire me" and "Make it magic") may be written by Apple's on-device language model. Your text and the suggestions stay on your device and are not sent to us or to Apple.

On-device video captions. In the video editor, Captions turns what's said in your video into captions with Apple's speech recognition, on your device. From iOS 26 it uses Apple's on-device speech models; each language's model is downloaded once from Apple, and no audio or text is sent with that download. Otherwise it uses Apple's speech recognizer only for languages your device can recognise without the internet, and iOS first asks for your permission to use speech recognition. The sound of your video and the caption text stay on your device and are not sent to us or to Apple. Captions are kept only while you edit and in the videos you save or share.

Photo access. You pick photos with Apple's system photo picker, so Aybuke can see only the photos you choose, not your whole library. To save results to your Photos library, the app asks for add-only permission, which lets it add images but not read your library.

Camera access. Retro Cam and Photo Booth use your camera only while they're open, after you allow camera access. The live preview and the effects run on your iPhone, and your shots stay on your device until you save or share them.

Backups. Data stored by the app may be included in your device backups (for example iCloud Backup), depending on your settings. Apple handles backups under its own terms.

You can delete your library, including Recently deleted, in Settings → Delete all creations. It's also deleted when you delete your account or the app.

5. Data we process on our servers

Anonymous account

When you first use a cloud AI feature or make a purchase, the app creates an anonymous account with our backend provider (Supabase). The account is identified by a random user ID. We don't collect your name, email address, phone number or password.

Optional account (Sign in with Apple)

Signing in is optional, and not every version of the app offers it. If you sign in with Sign in with Apple where the app offers it (so your credits follow you to another device; an active subscription stays with the Apple Account that bought it), Apple confirms your identity and our backend provider receives a stable Apple user identifier and, if you choose to share it, your email address, which can be a private relay address that forwards to you. We don't ask for your name and never see your Apple ID password. The anonymous account you used on that iPhone is merged into your account: paid credits, subscription records, job records and reports move over, and the anonymous account is deleted. We also keep a token from Apple so that, when you delete your account, we can revoke Aybuke's access to your Apple ID, as Apple requires. Your photos and library are not synced and stay on each iPhone.

Content you send to cloud AI features

When you use Magic Edit, Styles, AI Enhance, Erase or Create from text, the app sends the content needed for that request to our backend, which forwards it to our AI provider. This means your photo, the mask you brushed (for Erase) and your text prompt (for Create from text and Magic Edit). Section 6 explains what happens to it.

Job records

For each cloud AI job, we keep a record with: a job ID, your anonymous user ID, the type of operation, the template ID, the name of the AI model endpoint, the credit cost, the status, any error code and message, retry flags, the provider's request ID and timestamps. Job records also note whether free credits paid for the job, an estimate of what the AI provider charges for it and the hash of your App Store download (below), or, without a verified download, a salted hash of your network address. Job records don't contain the photo you sent or your prompt. Until you download them, they hold your results, which are deleted within 1 hour. We also use them to apply limits on how many jobs an account can start per minute, hour and day.

Credits

Your credit wallet (purchased credits), your subscription's credits with their period dates (kept with the subscription's entitlement record, below) and your credit ledger (holds and refunds for each job; credit pack grants with the Apple transaction ID; credits earned by watching an ad, with the ad's transaction ID; and credits taken back after Apple refunds a credit pack, or returned if Apple reverses that refund, with the pack's Apple transaction ID).

Your App Store download (free credits)

Free daily credits belong to your download of Aybuke from the App Store, not to an anonymous account, so they can't be collected by making new accounts. When you use a cloud AI feature, read your credit balance, buy a credit pack or ask for a rewarded ad, the app sends our server the app transaction that Apple signs for your download (Apple's "AppTransaction": it includes Apple's app transaction ID, the app's bundle ID and version, and the date of your first download). Apple gives each Apple Account one app transaction ID for Aybuke, the same on all its devices and after reinstalling. Our server checks Apple's signature and keeps only:

  • a one-way, salted hash of the app transaction ID (never the ID itself or the signed app transaction);
  • that day's free daily credits, including credits earned by watching ads;
  • the App Store environment (App Store, or TestFlight and App Review) and when the hash was first and last used; and
  • if our safety filters blocked several of your requests or results, those blocks and any resulting pause (section 9).

While your account exists, your job records, credit ledger and ad reward records point to this hash. When you delete your account, those links go, and the hash with its counts stays, no longer linked to any account, for the periods in section 14, only to prevent abuse of free credits and of our safety system. If Apple's confirmation isn't available, free credits can't be used, but purchased and subscription credits still work.

Ads for free members

If you don't have Pro and you're 13 or over, Aybuke shows ads from Google AdMob (Google LLC) on iPhone and iPad, in two places only:

  • Rewarded ads, always optional. When you're out of credits, you can choose to watch a short ad for 1 credit, a limited number of times a day (the app shows how many; the limit counts per account and per App Store download). Before the ad, the app asks our server for a single-use reward code, linked to your anonymous user ID and valid for 30 minutes. The app passes only this code to Google, never your user ID. When the ad has played to the end, Google sends our server a signed confirmation with the code, the ad unit, the reward, a transaction ID and the time, and we add the credit to your download's free daily credits. We keep a record of each confirmed reward (transaction ID, user ID, the hash of your App Store download, ad unit, reward and date) to give each reward only once and to apply the daily limit.
  • An occasional ad after you save. Now and then, an ad may appear after you save or share a finished creation: never in your first day with the app or before your third save, never while you're editing, at most one every 4 minutes, 3 a day, and none within 4 minutes of a rewarded ad. The app keeps these counts on your device (section 4).

What Google receives. When an ad is requested or shown, Google's ad software in the app sends Google information about your device and the app (such as the device model, operating system and app version, language and screen size), your IP address (from which Google estimates a general location, such as your country or city), identifiers that Google's software uses only within this app (not your device's advertising identifier, which the app never accesses), and how you interact with the ads (such as which ads were shown, taps, and how much of a video played), together with performance data. For rewarded ads it also receives the single-use reward code. Google never receives your photos, prompts, results, name, email address or user ID from us.

How we limit it. Every ad request is non-personalised: no interest-based or remarketing ads, and Google's restricted data processing is switched on. If you're under 18, or we don't know your age, requests are tagged for users under the age of consent and only ads suitable for a general audience are shown; adults can see ads rated up to "parental guidance". Google's ad software starts only when an ad may be shown, never for Pro members or anyone under 13, and never on a Mac.

Consent in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland. Before the first ad, adults there see Google's consent form, which explains Google's use of information stored on your device and lets you accept or decline. If you're under the age of consent, or we don't know your age, you aren't asked and get only limited, non-personalised ads. You can change your choice at any time in Settings → Ad privacy choices.

Google processes this information to show and measure ads and to prevent fraud and abuse, partly as an independent controller under Google's Privacy Policy. How Google uses information from apps that use its services explains more. Credits earned from ads have no cash value, can't be exchanged or transferred, and, like the free daily credits, are spent first and expire at the end of the day (00:00 UTC).

Purchases and subscriptions

Apple processes all payments. We never receive your card details, your name or your Apple Account email address.

To verify purchases, we receive Apple-signed transaction data, including transaction IDs, the product ID, purchase and expiry dates, the environment (sandbox or production) and an app account token that equals your anonymous user ID. Apple keeps this token with its purchase records under Apple's own privacy policy.

For subscriptions, we keep an entitlement record: the Apple original transaction ID, the product ID, the expiry date, a revoked flag (set when Apple refunds or revokes the subscription), the environment, whether the current period is a free trial, and the subscription's credit balance with its period dates, linked to your anonymous user ID.

Refunds and revocations. Apple sends our server signed notifications about Aybuke purchases (App Store Server Notifications), including when Apple refunds a purchase, revokes it (for example when Family Sharing access to it ends) or reverses a refund. Each notification contains Apple-signed transaction data like the data described above. Our server checks Apple's signature and uses the transaction IDs to find the matching record: a refunded or revoked subscription is marked as revoked, so Pro ends; a refunded credit pack's unused credits are taken back; and a reversed refund undoes the change. Other notifications change nothing. So that each notification counts only once, we keep only its notification ID, its type and subtype, the App Store environment, the product ID, what it changed and when it arrived, with no transaction ID or user ID (section 14).

To understand how our subscriptions perform (trials, renewals, cancellations, refunds), once you make a purchase (or if your Apple Account already has purchases with us) the app also shares the same purchase records with our subscription analytics provider, RevenueCat, together with your anonymous user ID and basic device and app information (app version, iOS version, country and language). RevenueCat never receives your photos, prompts or results.

Reports

If you use "Report this image", we receive the reason you select, any details you choose to add, the related job ID, your anonymous user ID and timestamps.

Server and security logs

Our hosting provider records technical logs when the app connects to our servers, such as IP address, request metadata and timestamps. To limit abuse across accounts, our server also counts cloud AI requests per network address for each hour, using a one-way, salted hash of the IP address instead of the address itself; these counts are deleted after a day.

When you contact us

If you email us, we receive your email address, the content of your message and anything you attach.

6. How cloud AI processing works

  1. Your consent comes first. Before any photo or prompt leaves your device for one of our cloud AI features, the app shows an opt-in screen that names the companies that process it. Nothing is sent until you agree. If you're under the age required by law, a parent or guardian must give or approve this consent (sections 3, 17 and 19). You can withdraw consent at any time in Settings → Cloud AI processing, and on-device features keep working.
  2. Upload. The app sends your input over an encrypted connection to our backend (Supabase Edge Functions; hosting region: EU (Frankfurt)).
  3. Safety checks. Your prompt is checked by our safety filters before the request is processed (section 9).
  4. AI processing. Our backend passes your input to our AI inference provider for that request only. By default, this is xAI (X.AI LLC and its affiliates, USA), which runs its Grok Imagine image models. If we change providers, jobs may instead go to fal.ai (Features & Labels, Inc., USA), which runs third-party models (where fal.ai uses a model developer's own service to run a model, for example Google for its Gemini image models, that developer also processes the request), or directly to Google (Gemini API) or OpenAI (Images API). The current list is in section 12.
  5. No storage of inputs. We don't store your photo, mask or prompt. We use xAI's Zero Data Retention option, under which xAI deletes requests and results within 1 hour and keeps no stored copies. When fal.ai is used, we instruct it not to keep request history, which means no storage of inputs or outputs.
  6. Delivery. The result is checked by automated safety filters and held temporarily in our database or on the provider's content delivery network, only so the app can download it. It is deleted as soon as the app confirms the download, and after 1 hour at the latest.
  7. On your device. If you keep the result, it's saved to your library on your iPhone.

We use only provider plans whose terms don't allow using customer content to train their models without our permission, and we never give that permission. We never use your photos, prompts or results to train AI models, sell them, or use them for advertising, marketing or data mining.

Create with Image Playground (Apple). On devices that support it, Create also offers Create with Image Playground, Apple's own image maker. It's Apple's feature, not one of our cloud AI features, so our consent screen and credits don't apply. Your description goes to Apple's Image Playground, which makes the image on your device or, from iOS 27, on Apple's Private Cloud Compute servers, under Apple's terms and with Apple's own usage limits; on those versions the app tells you before the first use. The app allows only Apple's illustration styles and turns off the option to use people from your photo library. Aybuke receives only the finished image, which is kept in your library on your device and is never sent to us.

Please don't upload photos or prompts containing sensitive information you don't want processed.

Other people in your photos. Your photos may show other people. We process their images in the same way as yours: only to perform the edit, briefly, and never to identify them. 9. Safety filters and automated decisions

To keep Aybuke safe, we use automated safety measures:

  • prompt filters in the app and on our servers that block, for example, nudity and sexual content, "undressing" or nudification requests, and any sexual content involving minors;
  • our AI providers' safety settings; and
  • an automated check of every output for nudity or sexual content, and, where we switch it on, a second automated check of the photo and the results by our AI provider's vision model.

If a filter blocks a request or a result, you aren't charged, and any credits are refunded. We record that it happened (the filter's category and the time, never the content), linked to your anonymous account and the hash of your App Store download (or, without a verified download, a salted hash of your network address). If our servers' filters block 3 of your requests or results within 24 hours, cloud AI pauses for 24 hours for that account and download; after 3 pauses within 30 days, we switch cloud AI off for that download until a human has reviewed it. On-device features keep working.

These filters and pauses are the only automated decisions we make about your requests. They affect only access to cloud AI features for a limited time, don't have legal or similarly significant effects on you within the meaning of Article 22 GDPR, and we don't profile you. If you think a filter blocked something by mistake, or a pause shouldn't apply to you, contact us at hello@buaze.com and a human will review it.

14. How long we keep data

  • Photos, masks and prompts sent to cloud AI features: not stored by us. They are passed to the AI provider for that request only.
  • Generated results in the cloud: only until the app confirms the download, and at most 1 hour, in our database or on the provider's content delivery network. Then they are deleted.

  • Credit wallet and credit ledger: as long as your account exists. Deleted when you delete your account.
  • Entitlement records: as long as your account exists. Deleted when you delete your account.
  • App Store notifications (the notification ID, its type and subtype, the App Store environment, the product ID, what it changed and when it arrived; no transaction ID or user ID): 30 days, then deleted. They aren't linked to your account, so deleting your account doesn't change this.
  • Anonymous account (user ID): as long as your account exists. Deleted when you delete your account, or when it's merged into your account after Sign in with Apple.
  • Account (Apple user identifier, email or relay address, Apple token): as long as your account exists. When you delete your account, the token is revoked with Apple and everything is deleted.
  • Subscription analytics records at RevenueCat: as long as your account exists. When you delete your account, we ask RevenueCat to delete them, including the records of anonymous user IDs merged into your account; RevenueCat completes the deletion shortly after. If RevenueCat can't take the request at that moment, we keep only the random user IDs concerned in a deletion queue and retry until RevenueCat confirms.
  • Reports: up to 12 months, for safety review and to improve our filters. Then deleted, or earlier if you delete your account.
  • Rewarded ads: single-use reward codes are valid for 30 minutes and deleted a day after they expire; records of confirmed rewards are kept for 30 days, then deleted, or earlier if you delete your account. Google keeps the ad data it receives under its own policies (section 11).
  • Hashed App Store download (the hash with the day's free credits, the environment and dates): deleted 30 days after the app last used it, unless cloud AI is switched off for that download (below).
  • Safety records (blocked requests and results, as category and time; pauses): deleted after 30 days. A switch-off after repeated pauses is kept until we lift it, and at most 12 months.
  • Hashed network address counts: deleted after a day. Without a verified App Store download, job and safety records use a salted hash of your network address in place of the download hash, for the periods above.
  • The last three are kept even after you delete your account, only to prevent abuse of free credits and of our safety system. After deletion they aren't linked to any account.
  • Server and security logs: a short period, typically no more than 90 days, for security and abuse prevention. They are deleted on this schedule even after you delete your account.
  • Emails and privacy requests: as long as needed to handle your request and to document how we handled it, unless we must keep them longer to establish, exercise or defend legal claims.
  • Data on your device: until you delete it, delete your account or delete the app.

We may keep data for longer where the law requires it or where it's needed for legal claims, for example information we must preserve when we report illegal content to the authorities.
